How the preview window behaves

Every state the window itself can be in, what holds each one open, what it accepts, and what ends it. Menus, buttons and the loading ring are out of scope.

17 states 19 transitions 8 terminators 6 known edges

Every item here has a permanent ID — states S01–S17, transitions T01–T19, terminators K1–K8, button-map rows B1–B2, known edges E1–E6, and the governing rule R1. Cite the ID rather than quoting.

IDs are permanent: new items get new numbers, and a removed item's ID is retired rather than reused. The canonical copy is INTERACTION.md in the repository — if the two ever disagree, that one is right.

R1
One rule underneath all of it: exactly one thing holds the window open at a time, and leaving that one thing ends the window.

While it is a hover preview, that thing is the source image — never the window, which is invisible to the pointer. Once you drag it, that thing becomes the window — the image stops mattering entirely. Once you pin it, nothing holds it but your explicit dismissal. Every hover bug this script has had came from two things holding it at once.

The escalation ladder

Three held states, each one gesture apart

Precondition hover Pointer lands on an eligible image
then resolve Probe candidates for a bigger original
held by image open Transient. Pointer-transparent.
drag > 3px detached Held by the window. Hit-testable.
click pinned Modal. Zoom and pan surface.

The ladder only goes one way. You can jump straight from open to pinned with a click, or step through detached first with a drag — but nothing demotes a window except closing it and starting again.

Before anything can open

What has to be true first

None of the states below are reachable unless the pointer is over an eligible element. That means: an <img> or an element with a CSS background image; displayed at least minDisplayed (48px) and no larger than maxDisplayed (off by default); not a video, a player, or a thumbnail inside a link that plainly points at one; the site not blacklisted; and, in modifier mode, the modifier key held. Nothing is decided in advance — all of it is evaluated the moment the pointer arrives.

Enumeration

The states

S01Waiting

idle

No window, no timer, no memory of anything. The resting state.

On screen
Nothing.
Ends on
Pointer entering an eligible image.
S02Waiting

armed

The pointer is on an eligible image and the hoverDelay timer (120 ms) is running. Nothing has been requested yet.

On screen
Nothing.
Held by
The pointer being on the image.
Ends on
Leaving the image — silently, with no trace.
S03Working

resolving, silent

Probes are in flight. For the first 150 ms nothing appears at all, so a cached or instant hit never flashes a ring at you.

On screen
Nothing.
Held by
The pointer being on the image.
Ends on
A result, exhaustion, or leaving the image.
S04Working

resolving, visible

Still searching past 150 ms. The ring appears near the cursor and trails it. There is still no window.

On screen
The ring, following the pointer.
Held by
The pointer being on the image.
Ends on
The first hit (opens a window), no hit at all, or leaving the image.
S05Open · held by the image

hover-held

The window is up. It is placed once, beside the pointer and nudged just far enough that the pointer sits ~10px inside its edge. It never moves again on its own and never follows the pointer.

On screen
The window. Pointer-transparent — clicks, hovers and scrolls pass straight through it to the page beneath.
Held by
The pointer being on the source image. Nothing else.
Accepts
Left press+release → pin. Left press+drag → detach. Right press → dismiss. (Default button map.)
Ends on
Leaving the image, immediately — no grace period, even though the window is sitting under your cursor.
S06Open · held by the image

hover-held, upgrading

Same as above, but the search is still running because a bigger original may exist. The picture can be swapped in place beneath you.

On screen
The window, plus the ring docked into its lower-right corner — the only signal that what you are looking at is not final.
On upgrade
The frame keeps its centre; only the pixels change.
Ends on
Same as hover-held. Leaving mid-search does not abort the loads already in flight — they finish and warm the cache.
S07Placed · held by the window

detached

You dragged it more than 3px. It has been deliberately positioned, so it stops tracking the image and starts answering to the pointer directly.

On screen
The window, now hit-testable — it intercepts the pointer instead of passing it through.
Held by
The pointer being on the window. The source image is now irrelevant to it.
Accepts
Click → pin. Drag → move it again.
Ends on
The pointer leaving the window — and the source image is suppressed, so moving back onto it does not re-open anything.
S08Placed · held by the window

detached, upgrading

A detached window whose search has not finished. Dragging never stops the search.

On screen
The window plus the docked ring.
On upgrade
Frame centre held; the window does not jump under your hand.
Ends on
Same as detached.
S09Transient

dragging (move), unpinned

The button is down and the window is following the pointer 1:1. All hover logic is suspended for the duration — a fast drag can outrun the frame, and the page elements sliding underneath must not be allowed to cancel it.

On screen
The window, moving. Clamped to stay 4px inside the viewport.
Ends on
Release. Past 3px of travel it lands in detached and the release does not pin.
S10Pinned · modal

pinned

A click promoted it. The backdrop now swallows clicks meant for the page, an X appears, and the keyboard and wheel belong to the window.

On screen
The window, the X, and the backdrop (dimmed only if dimOpacity is above 0).
Held by
Nothing. It outlives hover, scrolling and focus loss entirely.
Accepts
Wheel → zoom about the pointer. + / − → zoom about the centre. 0 → back to the opening scale. Arrows → pan (Shift for 3×). Drag the status bar → move the frame.
Ends on
The X, a click on the backdrop, Escape — or a right-click, under the default button map.
S11Pinned · geometry

pinned, frame growing

The image at the current scale is still smaller than the viewport cap, so zooming in enlarges the frame. The picture always fits; there is nothing to pan.

Cursor
Default. Dragging the image does nothing.
Floor
Zooming out stops at the scale the window opened at.
Ends on
The frame reaching maxWidthPct / maxHeightPct (92%), which promotes it to the state below.
S12Pinned · geometry

pinned, image spilling

The frame has hit the viewport cap and is now fixed. Further zoom pushes the image out past the frame's edges, which is the moment panning starts to mean something.

Cursor
grab over the image.
Accepts
Drag the image to pan; arrows to pan; wheel keeps the pixel under the pointer where it is.
Ceiling
maxZoom (32×), hard-capped at 64×.
S13Pinned · transient

dragging (pan)

Press on the image while it is spilling. The picture moves inside a frame that stays put.

Cursor
grabbing.
Bounds
Clamped so the frame never shows past the edges of the picture.
Ends on
Release.
S14Pinned · transient

dragging (move)

Press on the status bar. The whole frame moves; the picture stays put inside it. This is the only way to move a pinned window — which is why hiding the status bar also removes it.

Cursor
move over the bar.
Ends on
Release.
S15Pinned · working

pinned, upgrading

Pinning is a reason to keep looking, not to stop, so the search runs on. The swap is engineered to be invisible.

On upgrade
Three things are held constant: the frame's centre, its on-screen size, and the fraction of the picture sitting at the frame's middle. Only the pixels improve.
S16Gone

suppressed

No window — and the image it came from is blocked from opening another one. Entered by dismissing with a right-click, or by a detached window losing the pointer.

On screen
Nothing.
Why
Without it, the very next mouse movement over the same image would just re-open what you had deliberately got rid of.
Ends on
The pointer leaving that image and coming back. Other images are unaffected.
S17Gone

fading out

The window has been ended and is running out its fadeMs (90 ms) opacity transition. It is already inert.

Accepts
Nothing. It is not hit-testable and holds no state.
Then
~60 ms later the decoded image is released, so a long session does not accumulate bitmaps.

Terminators

Everything that closes an unpinned window

K1leaveThe pointer leaves whatever is holding it — the image, or the window once detached.
K2scrollAny page scroll. A detached window dies too; only pinning survives scrolling.
K3blurThe browser window losing focus.
K4resizeResizing the browser window. Pinned windows re-fit instead.
K5EscapeCloses it, but does not suppress the image.
K6mousedownA press anywhere on the page outside the window's rectangle.
K7key releaseIn modifier mode, releasing the modifier key.
K8button heldNo new window opens at all while a mouse button is down.

The left button always pans or moves a pinned window, whichever way the setting is pointed — otherwise a pinned frame could have no way to be moved. That last cell is the one place the browser's native context menu is still reachable over the window.

Known edges

Consequences worth knowing about

  1. A click on a hovered image usually pins instead of following the link. The window is invisible to the pointer, but a click inside its rectangle is still claimed by geometry — and since the window is nudged to touch your cursor, your cursor is nearly always inside it. This is the price of click-to-pin. If you want the page's own click, move off the image first.
  2. Escape is not the same as a right-click dismiss. Escape hides the window but does not suppress the image, so crossing into a child element and back can re-open it. Right-click is the one that stays down.
  3. Scrolling kills a detached window. Dragging a window aside to read the page under it does not survive a scroll — pin it if you want that.
  4. The window never follows the pointer. It is placed once, when it opens. Moving around within the same image does not reposition it.
  5. A hover-held window cannot be clicked on. Anything living inside it is out of reach until you detach or pin, because moving toward it means leaving the image, which ends it.
  6. Leaving mid-search does not cancel the network. Probes already in flight finish and populate the cache — which is why an image that "did nothing" the first time can open instantly a moment later. That is a cache warming up, not a fixed bug.
